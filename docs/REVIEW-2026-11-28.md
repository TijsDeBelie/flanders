# Core mechanics review (2026-11-28)

What to improve next, including the core rules. Each finding names the code it rests on and, where possible, a measurement. The proposals are ranked by value for effort; numbers in them are first guesses to be tuned with the audit below.

**How this was done.** Read the rules in `crates/game-core` (food, growth, happiness, seasons, raids, market, wild places), the earlier [balance review](balance-review.md) and [combat review](combat-review.md), and re-ran `web/tools/balance-audit.mjs` (12 seeds, the real core, the opening on its own and a 40-day first year with no orders after the opening). Web search for comparable games found guides, wikis and reviews, no player data, so the "why" is a hypothesis: [Farthest Frontier guide](https://www.farthestfrontier.com/guide/), [Farthest Frontier desirability (TechRaptor)](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/farthest-frontier-desirability-guide), [Manor Lords development (Hooded Horse wiki)](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Manor_Lords/Special:MyLanguage/development), [Manor Lords trade rework (PCGamesN)](https://www.pcgamesn.com/manor-lords/experimental-trade-rework), [Against the Storm hostility (wiki)](https://hoodedhorse.com/wiki/Against_the_Storm/Hostility), [Against the Storm impatience (wiki)](https://hoodedhorse.com/wiki/Against_the_Storm/Queen%27s_Impatience).

## What the measurements say

Opening (two days, before raids), 12 sites:

| Opening | Population | Meals left | Sites with an empty larder | Hunger deaths |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hall only | 8 | 0.75–34.5 | 12 | 2 |
| One house, one farm | 13 | 6–79.5 | **10** | 0 |
| Two houses, one farm | 18 | 5–20 | **12** | 2 |
| One house, two farms | 13 | 313–450 | 0 | 0 |
| Farm + bakery + woodcutter | 12–13 | 117–275 | 0 | 0 |
| Farm + hunt / pasture | 13 | 6–79.5 | 10 | 0 |
| Farm + fishing hut | not buildable on any of the 12 sites | | | |

First year (40 days, natural raids, fires, plague, no orders after the opening): every opening lost villagers to hunger on 6 to 12 of the 12 sites and to war on 10 to 12; nobody died of cold. "One farm" ended at 8–13 people with hunger deaths on all 12 sites; "two farms" on 6.

The [balance review](balance-review.md) reported 0 empty larders for the one-farm and farm-plus-meat openings; they are now 10 of 12. Food got tighter since (raw grain fills half, meat is no longer a staple), and the first goals were not changed with it.

What the code says:
- **Growth is instant and only housing limits it.** While there is room and food, every tick a villager arrives with a chance of 1 in 4 to 1 in 8 (`step`, near `self.arrive(1)`): a new house fills in seconds. Happiness only switches this from 1-in-4 to 1-in-8 and stops births below 40.
- **Happiness has almost no teeth.** It sets taxes (`EX_TAX`) and gates births, nothing else: no slower work, nobody leaves for being unhappy (only for lack of housing), no unrest. It starts at 40 and anything above 40 barely matters.
- **The goals end at "Raise your castle"** (`web/src/goals.ts`, 17 goals). After that the only aim is clearing every camp.
- **Gold has few sinks.** Taxes, the mint, market and courthouse make it; soldiers cost nothing to keep (no wages anywhere in `battle.rs` / `military.rs`), so an army never weighs on the economy.
- **Decisions are four events** (`CH_PLAGUE`, `CH_PREACHER`, `CH_NOBLE`, `CH_WINTER`). There are no standing policies.

## Proposals, best value for effort first

### 1. Fix the opening (small, measured, high value)
- **Goal order:** the first goals ask for three houses and then a farm; the audit says two houses on one farm runs dry on 12 of 12 sites. Ask for a farm first, then two houses, then a second farm before more houses. Make the tutorial say the same.
- **A starting larder that lasts the first harvest:** the hall's kitchen garden (`BACKUP_*`) only tops up below a threshold. Either a bigger start stock of grain, or a first farm that comes in faster, so one farm and one house never runs dry before day 2.
- **The fishing hut is never buildable near the start** (0 of 12). Check whether the start sites have water in reach at all and, if they do, widen `shore_snap`'s search; otherwise say in its description that it needs a river or lake in your land.
- **Re-run the audit in CI** (opening only, about a minute) and fail when an opening that was fine starts starving. This would have caught the change above.

### 2. Happiness with teeth (core rules)
Today happiness is a number to keep above 40. The comparable games make satisfaction drive everyday work: Farthest Frontier's needs grow with the settlement (food variety, clothing, ale, spirituality) and unmet ones cost happiness and productivity; Manor Lords' approval sets growth and militia morale.
- **Growth from reputation, not seconds:** villagers arrive a few a day, more when happiness is high (for example 1 per day at 40 up to 4 at 90), so a new house is a promise, not an instant crowd. Births stay as they are.
- **Unhappiness has consequences:** below 30, one villager leaves a day (as for overcrowding); below 20, work slows (90%); above 70, work speeds up (105%), shown in the `work_pace` breakdown as a fourth factor.
- **Needs that grow with the house tier:** tier 1 wants food and firewood (today), tier 2 adds a second food (bread, fish or meat) and a well or market in reach, tier 3 adds cloth or ale and a chapel in reach. A house whose needs are met can be upgraded; an unmet need costs a little happiness. This adds two small chains (wool from pasture to a weaver; grain to a brewery) and makes the existing variety of food matter.
- Everything is in the core and the state hash already carries population and happiness inputs; the interface needs a "needs" line on the house panel.

### 3. Standing policies (small core change, many choices)
Four one-off events are all the decisions there are. Three or four standing decrees with a cost, each a toggle in the town hall (a command like `option`):
- **Taxes** low / normal / high: gold ±50% against happiness ±8.
- **Rations** lean / normal / generous: food eaten 80% / 100% / 120% against happiness −6 / 0 / +4 and births.
- **Corvée:** villagers work on building sites faster (+50% building speed) but happiness −5 while on.
- **Muster:** militia doubled in a raid, but farms work at 80% for the day after.

### 4. Wages and an army that costs something (core rules)
Soldiers eat but are free otherwise, so the safe play is the biggest army the barracks can train. A wage in gold per soldier per day (for example 1 per 4 soldiers at home, double in the field), unpaid soldiers desert one a day, and the Military tab shows the bill. Gold then has a steady sink, and the raids-from-camps pacing has a cost on both sides: an army to clear camps against fewer raids.

### 5. A goal after the castle: renown and titles (medium)
The goals end with the castle. A renown score from what the village has done (camps cleared, bosses killed, roads and mineshafts held, population, years survived), with titles from Lord to Baron, Count and Duke. Each title gives one charter to choose (a market charter, a mining charter, a military charter) and raises raids' strength a step, so the game keeps rising after the castle. It is visible to other players, so multiplayer has a ladder.

### 6. Raids an absent player can survive (balance)
The first-year audit is the worst case (no orders after the opening), but war killed villagers on 10 to 12 of 12 sites for every opening. A village that has built a watchtower and a few houses should hold off the first raid on its own. Options: the hall's guards scale with population for the first ten days, or the first raid is always a small one, or the bandit warning pauses the village by default (it does in the settings; the audit does not use it). Measure with the audit's `--year`.

### 7. Smaller things found on the way
- **Mule trains can be robbed:** a caravan from a shaft without a road could be waylaid by a live camp in reach (the robbers drawing in `travellers.ts` exists already).
- **The garrison on the Military tab**, with a button to reinforce it from the barracks.
- **AI agent tools** (`mcp/`) for the wild places: `visit_place`, `tithe`, `abandon_shaft`, `lay_shaft_road`.
- **`desync.test.mjs` is stale:** it expects `ResyncPolicy.MAX`, which no longer exists; it is not run in CI, so nobody noticed. Fix the test and add the file to `test:demo` in CI.
- **A flaky network test:** `net.test.mjs` "a player who reopens the room under a new id is recognised" failed once in CI on a commit that did not touch the network code and passes locally; worth a look at its simulated clock.

## Suggested order
1 (the opening) first: it is small, measured and every new player meets it. Then 2 and 3 together (happiness gives the decrees something to trade against), then 4, then 5. 6 can go alongside any of them; it needs only tuning and the audit.
